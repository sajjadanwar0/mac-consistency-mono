\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{%
\begin{abstract}
Agents in a multi-agent LLM system share state through memory stores and tool registries, and an agent's step spans an inference call of unbounded latency between its read and its commit. We formalize four concurrency anomalies for this regime---stale generation, phantom tool, causal cascade, tool-effect reordering---and name the classical condition each ports: stale generation is a failed commit-time read validation, and we prove (TLAPS) that its absence implies value-level consistency with the serial execution in commit order, a conditional guarantee over faithful histories whose hypotheses the artifact checks on every committed record. Each anomaly has a verified detector, each point of the $2^4$ exclusion lattice is realized by a checked runtime configuration, and along one chain $L_0\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq L_4$ the disciplines are verified in Verus, with the $L_1$, $L_2$ and $L_4$ bytes the ones measured, including the $L_1$ decision of the live study; every claim is labeled with its assurance level, from checked model to verified bytes. The evaluation is scored on outcomes, not flags. On an unmodified LangGraph runtime under live models, read-set validation removes every commit grounded in a superseded read at one re-spent generation per conflict, yet at a 150\,ms race offset the booking still contradicts the final trip date in $34\%$ and $7\%$ of sessions, because the revision landed after the commit; sweeping the race offset from $0$ to $1.5$\,s drives the predicate to $0$ and the residue to $52\%$. That residue is not a concurrency anomaly---it occurs in correct sequential executions---and no commit-time discipline reaches it; we define the residue predicate on effects and their recovered bases, show that under an open-world store it can only be owed, and realize a basis ledger that registers the obligation atomically (TLC-checked at every lattice point; invariant verified in Verus). On the same live study, with the commit decision taken by the verified store, the ledger with a rebooking compensator ends with $0/100$ wrong bookings on three API families where read validation alone leaves $12$--$63$. Per sound committed operation, snapshot isolation costs $+0.4\%$ to $+11.1\%$ in tokens and pessimistic locking up to $+97\%$. A census of $98$ public LangGraph repositories finds the stale-generation precondition in no graph site, which we report as a negative prevalence result, while a released tool executor batches an order-dependent pipeline on $30/30$ turns of one backbone. We state every number's provenance and every gap between predicate and harm.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A multi-agent LLM system shares state. One agent writes a plan that another
reads; a tool registry is consulted at planning time and called at execution
time; a supervisor retracts a decision that a worker has already acted on. Each
agent's step has the shape of a transaction---read, compute, commit---with one
difference from every setting in which transactions have been studied: the
compute phase is a neural-inference call that takes seconds to minutes, during
which the read set is neither locked nor cheaply re-validated. Deployed
frameworks place this phase inside a pinned snapshot (LangGraph's superstep), a
re-rendered context (Letta) or a message queue (AutoGen), and none of them
states what guarantee the shared state carries across it.

\subsection{A failure, constructed and then observed}
\label{sec:intro-example}
Two agents share a trip record. A flight agent reads ``trip date $=$ 14 June,''
drafts a reservation over a model call, and books it. During that call a second
agent, acting for the user, moves the trip to 21 June. The flight agent commits
a 14 June booking beside a 21 June trip date. No component failed; the history
is even serializable, in the order in which the flight agent runs to completion
and the revision follows. What it is not is serializable \emph{in the order in
which its operations committed}: the revision committed first, and a flight
agent executing after it would have read 21 June. \Cref{fig:window} draws the
shape.

We ran this scenario on an unmodified LangGraph runtime under live models
(\cref{sec:live-a1}). With the second agent started inside the first agent's
generation window, the framework committed the stale booking in every session
in which the revision landed inside the window---$\LiveGptVanillaAOne\%$ and
$\LiveHaikuVanillaAOne\%$ of sessions on two API models, the remainder landing
after the commit---and read-set validation at commit removed every such firing.
The same data record a second fact the predicate does not see: when the
revision lands \emph{after} the commit, the booking contradicts the final trip
date just the same, and no isolation discipline can prevent it. We report both
numbers throughout, because the distance between them is the distance between a
consistency predicate and the harm it is meant to track. The rates themselves
are properties of the race we opened (a $150$\,ms offset; swept from $0$ to
$1.5$\,s in \cref{fig:offset}) and we attach no prevalence reading to them.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{What this paper is not claiming.} A census of
\CensusRepos{} public LangGraph repositories (\cref{sec:prevalence}) finds
the structural precondition of the failure above---a reader and a writer of
one last-value channel scheduled together---in none of \CensusSites{} graph
sites. We take that at face value: public LangGraph code is mostly sequential,
and four of five frameworks we probed close the window by construction. The
regime studied here is the one in which contention is designed in (parallel
fan-out over a shared store, several agents acting for one user, a registry
mutated while plans are in flight), and the contribution is not that the
anomalies are common: it is a catalog with mechanized definitions,
disciplines priced per unit of sound work, and an account of what each
leaves behind.

\subsection{Why existing theory does not operationalize these failures}
\label{sec:why-classical-fails}
Three traditions bear on the problem and none applies as it stands. Hardware
models~\cite{Lamport1979SC,GharachorlooEtAl1990,PulteEtAl2018ARMv8,HerlihyWing1990}
presuppose bounded latency and static access sets; database isolation
theory~\cite{BerensonEtAl1995,Adya1999,AdyaLiskovOneil2000} supplies the
anomaly-by-anomaly method we follow but holds the read set under lock or
snapshot for the transaction's lifetime; replicated-storage
consistency~\cite{ViottiVukolic2016Consistency,LloydEtAl2011COPS,Burckhardt2014,TerryEtAl1994}
treats operations as discrete events with summarizable signatures. The agent
regime has unbounded generation latency, a tool registry that is first-class
mutable state, and irreversible external effects---so the cascading-abort
machinery of database recovery, which assumes effects can be undone, is
exactly what fails.

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}
We claim no new concurrency theory. Each anomaly below is a classical one,
and we say which. The contribution is the operational port of four of them to
agent runtimes, its mechanization at stated assurance levels, and an
evaluation that measures the gap between each predicate and the harm it
tracks---including the part of the harm that is not a concurrency anomaly at
all.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{(C1) A catalog with classical names, and the
residue it leaves} (\cref{sec:anomalies}). Four history predicates in
\TLA{}---stale generation (\A1), phantom tool (\A2), causal cascade (\A3),
tool-effect reordering (\A6)---each with a TLC witness and its classical
counterpart: a failed backward validation~\cite{KungRobinson1981OCC}, a
dangling capability reference, recoverability with externalization as the
commit point~\cite{BernsteinHadzilacosGoodman1987}, and order preservation
of external effects. \Cref{thm:corc} (TLAPS, \TlapsCommitOrder{}
obligations) places \A1-freedom among the classical classes exactly, as a
conditional guarantee whose hypotheses (per-agent non-overlap,
read-before-commit, faithful records) are stated, checked on every committed
record, and violated by one deployed construct we name. Beside the catalog
we define the \emph{residue predicate} \Rp{}: an irreversible effect is out
while ordinary later writes have revised the state it was derived from. \Rp{}
is not an anomaly---it fires in correct sequential executions---and we say so;
it is the harm no commit-time discipline reaches, decided on the records by
argument provenance, and on the live study \A1 and \Rp{} partition the
outcome column (\cref{sec:residue}).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{(C2) A lattice, realized} (\cref{sec:lattice}). The
$2^4$ exclusion lattice is trivial; what is not is that every point is inhabited
by a checked runtime configuration (\LatticeRuns{} TLC runs, no mismatch,
bounded as stated), that one chain $L_0\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq L_4$ has a
verified realizing runtime at every level, and that the basis ledger that
answers \Rp{} composes with every point without disturbing its verdict
(\LatticeThirtyTwoRuns{} further runs).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{(C3) Verified detectors and disciplines}
(\cref{sec:mechanization}). \VerusCurated{} Verus obligations, no
\texttt{assume} or \texttt{admit}, under two enumerated axioms and three
enumerated stubs: detectors sound and complete against their specifications,
two concurrent $L_1$ stores proved window-free under a verified reader--writer
lock, $L_2$ (output commit), $L_3$ (commit-order sequencing) and $L_4$
(validation at dispatch) runtimes whose $L_2$ and $L_4$ bytes are the ones
measured. \Cref{tab:assurance} labels every claim with its assurance
level (checked model, verified decision procedure, verified bytes,
unverified port, open) and names each correspondence between \TLA{}
definition, Verus restatement, executed code and scorer, with its hypothesis
and the gate that checks it on the data.
We account for the obligations by what they bear (supplement \S\,S):
\VerusLoadBearing{} underpin a deployed or measured artifact and
\VerusScaffold{} are scaffolding. The basis ledger that answers \Rp{} is
\LfiveVerusIntro{} in Verus: its invariant is preserved by every step and
excludes the residue witness; its executable form in the live study is
harness code, labeled as such.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{(C4) An evaluation scored on outcomes, not flags}
(\cref{sec:eval}). For each discipline we report the anomaly it excludes, the
harm that remains, and the price: on the live LangGraph study, the $L_1$
residue is \Rp{} in $\LiveGptValidatedWrong\%$ and
$\LiveHaikuValidatedWrong\%$ of sessions at the $150$\,ms offset, decided on
the records; with the commit decision taken by the verified $L_1$ store
through a $100$-line bridge the residue is the same kind of number
(\LiveVerifiedOutcome), and the basis ledger with a compensator removes it
(\LfiveLiveOutcome) at one compensation generation per supersession; on
$1{,}000$ seeded cascades per
depth, output commit removes exposure exactly when the review precedes the
release and refuses the retraction otherwise; per sound committed operation,
snapshot isolation costs $+0.4$--$11.1\%$ and pessimistic locking
$+1.2$--$97\%$ in tokens, with a wall-clock measurement whose run-order
confound we quantify rather than assume away.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{(C5) Where the preconditions occur} (\cref{sec:prevalence}).
A census of \CensusRepos{} public LangGraph repositories (\CensusSites{} graph
sites) finds no co-scheduled reader and writer of a last-value channel---a
negative result for \A1's structural precondition, which we report as one---while
a released tool executor dispatches a model-emitted, order-dependent pipeline
concurrently on $\ToolNodeGptBatched/\ToolNodeGptBatched$ turns of one backbone
and never on another, which is the case for \A6.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{What this paper does not establish.} It does not
establish a production prevalence for any anomaly: every occurrence figure is
a race our harness opened or a rate on our own workloads, and the census
measures how public code is written, not how often it runs. It does not
verify the instrumentation or the Python harnesses (trust base:
\cref{sec:trust}). And a verified arm's zero is a theorem executing, not
evidence, so the evaluation leads with costs and with counts that read no
runtime flag.

\section{Runtime model}
\label{sec:model}

A system consists of a finite set of agents $A$, a shared memory $M$ mapping
cells to values with a distinguished sentinel $\mathrm{NULL}$, and a shared
registry $R$ of available tools. An agent executes a sequence of
\emph{operations}, each in three phases: a \emph{read} phase that captures the
values of a chosen subset of cells (the operation's $\rs$) together with the
registry contents ($\rreg$), stamping the read with the current log length as
$\rt$; a \emph{generation} phase, dominated by inference latency and not
treated as instantaneous; and a \emph{write} phase that atomically appends a
record to the log, installing the planned writes ($\ws$, $\wv$), the selected
tool ($\pt$) and the registry at commit ($\wreg$), stamped $\wt$. The log
length is a logical clock~\cite{Lamport1978TimeClocks}; a history $h$ is the
log, $h\in\mathrm{Seq}(\fld{OpRecord})$. The specification is
\texttt{Memory.tla}.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Store axiom.} A read returns the latest committed
value at or before its read time. TLC confirms this and the two hypotheses
below as invariants of \texttt{Memory.tla} over all
\CommitOrderMemoryStates{} reachable states at the checked bounds
(\cref{sec:a1}).

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Per-agent non-overlap; read before commit.} Two
operations of one agent do not overlap in $[\rt,\wt]$; every operation reads
strictly before it commits. Both are invariants of the model, and both are
load-bearing for \cref{thm:corc} and for the detector transcription of
\cref{sec:verus-detectors}. A framework that runs one node as several parallel
instances under one name (LangGraph's \texttt{Send} fan-out) violates the first
and is outside the model; \cref{sec:prevalence} counts how often that shape
appears in public code.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Determinism is not assumed by the theorems.} Every
mechanized result ranges over recorded histories with write values free. Under
sampling, a value mismatch need not change the downstream decision, so the
\A1 detector is a sound candidate screen that over-reports by a factor
\cref{sec:materiality} measures. Durable-execution engines take the same
stance~\cite{BurckhardtEtAl2021Durable}: replay is deterministic because every
non-deterministic step's result is recorded, not because the step is.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Atomic commit is at the record level.} External
effects are outside the store and are governed separately: their order by
\A6 and $L_3$, their release relative to a still-retractable basis by \A3 and
$L_2$. At-most-once externalization is assumed and not modeled; \cref{sec:limitations}
reports a deployed resumption path that violates it.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{No replication.} The memory is one logical store.
Split view under replication (\A4) is formalized separately at the Verus
model level (supplement \S\,J) and plays no role in the lattice.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Placement.} The model does not derive from any one
runtime. LangGraph is driven through it live (\cref{sec:live-a1}); three
others are placed informally (supplement \S\,D).

\subsection{\A1: stale generation}
\label{sec:a1}
\begin{definition}[Stale generation]
\label{def:a1}
A history $h$ exhibits \emph{stale generation} when there exist
$i\neq j$ with $h[i].\fld{agent}\neq h[j].\fld{agent}$ and
$c\in h[i].\rs\cap h[j].\ws$ such that
\[
  \begin{gathered}
  h[i].\rt < h[j].\wt < h[i].\wt\\
  \text{and}\quad h[i].\rv[c] \neq h[j].\wv[c].
  \end{gathered}
\]
\end{definition}

\noindent\emph{Witness.} TLC finds a five-state counterexample: $a_2$ reads
$c_1=\mathrm{NULL}$ at $\rt=0$; $a_1$ commits $c_1=v_1$ at $\wt=1$; $a_2$
commits at $\wt=2$ with $\rv[c_1]=\mathrm{NULL}$.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Classical placement.} The minimal witness is a single
read--write antidependency from $i$ to $j$, serializable in the order $i;j$,
and none of Adya's proscribed phenomena~\cite{Adya1999}; TLC exhibits such a
history (claim \texttt{A1OnlyOnNonSerializable}, violated as expected). \A1
is therefore not an isolation anomaly in the classical sense. Its
\emph{absence} is nevertheless a classical correctness class, and we state
which one at the level at which the theorem holds.

\begin{definition}[Commit-order read consistency]
\label{def:corc}
A history is \emph{commit-order read consistent} when every operation read, for
every cell in its read set, exactly the value it would have read had it
executed atomically at its own commit point: for all $i$ and $c\in h[i].\rs$,
$h[i].\rv[c]$ is the value of the latest write to $c$ with
$\wt<h[i].\wt$, or $\mathrm{NULL}$ if there is none.
\end{definition}

Writes are installed in commit order in any case, so \cref{def:corc} is
value-level equivalence, on the reads, to the serial execution in commit
order. It is the value-level analogue of Weikum and Vossen's
commit-order-preserving serializability~\cite{WeikumVossen2001} and not that
condition itself, which is conflict-based; under value-unique writes the two
coincide on reads (view equivalence~\cite{Papadimitriou1979}).

\begin{theorem}[\A1-freedom implies commit-order read consistency]
\label{thm:corc}
Let $h$ satisfy the store axiom, read-before-commit and per-agent non-overlap.
If $h$ has no \A1 witness, then $h$ is commit-order read consistent.
\end{theorem}

The proof (\texttt{CommitOrder.tla}; TLAPS, \TlapsCommitOrder{} obligations,
no omitted step) is Kung and Robinson's backward-validation argument run
against our definitions: if some operation's read of $c$ disagrees with the
latest write to $c$ before its commit, that write lies inside the operation's
window, was made by another agent (one agent's operations do not overlap), and
wrote a different value---an \A1 witness. Fifteen TLC verdicts
(\texttt{check\_commitorder.sh}) fix what the statement says, and all fifteen
reproduce: over all \CommitOrderHistories{} histories of length at most three
the implication holds; it is not vacuous; it fails when either droppable
hypothesis is removed; the relational definitions equal
\texttt{Memory.tla}'s and a literal serial replay; the three hypotheses are
invariants of the operational model over \CommitOrderMemoryStates{} reachable
states; and the converse fails in exactly one shape---a value overwritten and
then restored inside the window fires \A1 although the history is
commit-order read consistent. The predicates nest strictly:
\[
  \neg\text{CORC} \;\Longrightarrow\; A_1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{validation fails}.
\]
\emph{Reach.} The theorem is a conditional guarantee over faithful histories
that satisfy its three hypotheses; it says nothing about serializability of
an application, about effects, about reads the instrumentation did not
declare, or about a runtime that violates per-agent non-overlap. One deployed
construct does: LangGraph's \texttt{Send} fan-out runs several instances
under one agent name and is outside the model (\cref{sec:model}). On the
committed data both droppable hypotheses hold---\HypSameAgentOverlaps{}
same-agent overlapping windows and \HypReadBeforeCommitViolations{} reads
not before their commit in \HypRecords{} records over \HypTraces{} traces
(\texttt{hypothesis\_gates.py}, a gate that fails on the first
violation)---and a runtime can enforce the first by refusing a second
operation of an agent whose window is open. The converse shape is a false positive of $L_1$, priced in
discarded generations; on the live records it occurs in \HypRestored{} of
\HypAOneWitnesses{} \A1 witness pairs.
Two corollaries are mechanized with the theorem. The classical lost update
(P4 of Berenson et al.~\cite{BerensonEtAl1995}) is an \A1 witness in which
the stale reader also writes the cell it read, so $L_1$ excludes it. A
\emph{blind} overwrite---a write by an operation that never read the cell---is
not an anomaly in isolation theory and is outside the catalog; it matters in
\cref{sec:prevalence}, because the one in-the-wild bug we reproduce is of that
kind, and in \cref{sec:live-a1}, because a revision that lands \emph{after}
the reader's commit is of that kind too.

\subsection{\A2: phantom tool}
\label{sec:a2}
\begin{definition}[Phantom tool]
\label{def:a2}
$h$ exhibits a \emph{phantom tool} when for some $i$,
\[
  \begin{gathered}
  h[i].\pt\neq\mathrm{NULL},\\
  h[i].\pt\in h[i].\rreg,\\
  h[i].\pt\notin h[i].\wreg.
  \end{gathered}
\]
\end{definition}
An agent selects a tool from the registry it read; the registry mutates
during generation; the commit emits a call to a capability that no longer
exists. TLC's witness has four states. \emph{Tool validity}, canonically, is
membership of the (identifier, signature) pair: a tool is the same tool while
both are unchanged. \Cref{def:a2} is stated over identifiers, the \TLA{}
registry being a set of them; the Verus restatement of \cref{sec:l4} maps
identifiers to signatures and fires additionally when a tool is re-signed
under its old name. The two coincide under the encoding the executable
dispatcher uses, in which re-signing a tool is removal of the old pair and
insertion of a fresh one, so that a re-signed tool is a removed tool to
\cref{def:a2}; the scorer and the $L_4$ dispatcher decide the pair form, and
\cref{tab:assurance} records the correspondence as an encoding, not a
proof. A tool the agent names that was never in $\rreg$ (a hallucinated
call) is not \A2 and not in the catalog.

\subsection{\A3: causal cascade}
\label{sec:a3}
When an operation is retracted in a compensating (saga-style) runtime, any
operation that read a value it produced is grounded on a basis no surviving
commit supports. The classical remedy, cascading abort, flags the dependent
aborted. In the agent regime that remedy fails in a specific way: if the
dependent's tool effects have already been issued, the flag changes nothing
that reached the world. The cataloged predicate is therefore stated over
externalization.

\begin{definition}[Causal cascade]
\label{def:a3}
Let each record carry $\fld{aborted}$ and $\fld{externalized}$ flags and a
predecessor set $\fld{preds}$ (the operations whose committed writes it
observed, transitively closed). $h$ exhibits a \emph{causal cascade} when for
some $j,p$:
\[
  h[j].\fld{externalized}\;\wedge\;p\in h[j].\fld{preds}\;\wedge\;h[p].\fld{aborted}.
\]
\end{definition}
The surviving-dependent form, $\neg h[j].\fld{aborted}\wedge p\in
h[j].\fld{preds}\wedge h[p].\fld{aborted}$, is kept beside it as
\textsc{CascadeUnpropagated}; a cascading abort falsifies that form by
flagging $j$ after $j$'s effects are out, and \cref{sec:l2-eval} measures the
separation on runnable arms.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Prevention is output commit, and output commit has a
residue.} Under \cref{def:a3} a runtime must hold an operation's effects until
every operation in its closure has released its own, and must refuse to
retract an operation whose effects are out. The first clause is prevention.
The second keeps the predicate false by refusing to set the flag it reads, and
we do not count that as prevention: a refused retraction is a basis someone
wished to withdraw after release, the ordinary rule of recoverability that a
committed transaction cannot abort, and every measurement in
\cref{sec:l2-eval} counts its residue beside the zero.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{What the detector decides.} A trace without
provenance fields cannot decide \cref{def:a3}. What it can decide is an
\emph{unsupported read}---a committed read of a value that no surviving
committed write produced at or before the read. The verified
\texttt{detect\_a3} decides exactly that screen, and its relation to
\cref{def:a3} is five mechanized facts (supplement \S\,G): it is not sound
in general (a value collision hides the aborted writer), it is sound under
value-unique writes, the hypothesis black-box isolation checkers also
need~\cite{Aphyr2020Elle,TanEtAl2020Cobra}, it holds along chains, it is not
complete, and it is blind to relabeled effects. We therefore claim
\cref{def:a3} only where the hypothesis holds, and check it: value-unique
writes hold by construction in the synthetic cascades (distinct value ranges
per writer) and in all \HypLiveLtwoSessions{} live replay sessions
(\HypLiveLtwoCollisions{} collisions), the two data sets the detector scores;
they fail in \HypCollisionCells{} cells of the pilot and occurrence-study
traces, where only \A1 is scored. A recorder that stamps each written value with its writer's operation
identifier makes the hypothesis a system invariant.

\subsection{\A6: tool-effect reordering}
\label{sec:a6}
Each record additionally carries an issuance order $\fld{io}$ and a commit
order $\fld{co}$ over its external effects; $\ws$ and $\wv$ derive from
$\fld{co}$.
\begin{definition}[Tool-effect reordering]
\label{def:a6}
$h$ exhibits \emph{tool-effect reordering} when for some $i$,
\[
  \begin{gathered}
  |h[i].\fld{io}|\ge 2,\quad h[i].\fld{co}\neq h[i].\fld{io},\\
  h[i].\fld{co}\ \text{is a permutation of}\ h[i].\fld{io}.
  \end{gathered}
\]
\end{definition}
The minimal witness (three states, one agent, two cells) is benign over store
cells, where writes to distinct cells commute; the predicate names an anomaly
only under the reading in which each element of $\fld{io}$ is an external
effect whose order an observer can see. It over-reports on commuting effects
and, because it requires a permutation, is silent on dropped and duplicated
effects; \cref{sec:limitations} exhibits the duplicated case in a deployed
framework.


\subsection{Snapshot insufficiency}
\label{sec:snapshot}
$L_1$ is not a snapshot guarantee: ``reads reflect memory at read time''
is the store axiom of \cref{thm:corc}, holds on every reachable log, and
TLC exhibits a history satisfying it on which \A1 fires
(\texttt{MC\_A1\_struct}; supplement \S\,B). The live instance is a runtime
that pins a consistent snapshot per node---LangGraph's superstep---and has
not thereby obtained $L_1$ (\cref{sec:live-a1}).

\subsection{What the catalog leaves: the residue predicate}
\label{sec:residue}
Every predicate above is decided at or before an operation's commit, and a
discipline that excludes all four still leaves a harm the live study measures
in a third of sessions (\cref{sec:live-a1}): the external effect a commit
released is irreversible, while the state it was derived from is ordinary
revisable state that any later operation may overwrite. This is not a
concurrency anomaly. It fires in correct sequential executions---book
against the date known at commit, then be contradicted by a legitimate
revision---and classical isolation has no name for it because a committed
transaction's inputs are final by definition; the nearest notions are view
maintenance~\cite{GuptaMumick1995ViewMaint} with the view outside the system,
and the saga's compensation~\cite{GarciaMolinaSalem1987Sagas}. We define it because an evaluation scored on outcomes needs a predicate
for the outcome's other half, and keep it outside the catalog and the
lattice.

Each operation record carries a \emph{basis}, the (cell, value) pairs its
external effect was derived from, and an $\fld{obligation}$ flag. The basis
is recovered by \emph{argument provenance}: a read value that appears
verbatim among the effect's arguments is part of its basis, and nothing is
declared by the agent. This is why-provenance~\cite{BunemanEtAl2001Why}
restricted to copying, with that tradition's known limit: a value that
reaches the effect through a computation leaves no basis, so the extractor
under-reaches and never over-reports. In the model the basis is a
nondeterministic subset of the read values, so every extractor is covered.

\begin{definition}[Residue predicate]
\label{def:residue}
$h$ exhibits a \emph{superseded-basis effect} (\Rp{}; \texttt{NoA7} in the
artifact) when for some $i$, $h[i].\fld{externalized}$,
$\neg h[i].\fld{obligation}$, and for some $(c,v)\in h[i].\fld{basis}$ the
\emph{latest} write to $c$ committed after $h[i].\wt$ wrote a value other
than $v$:
\[
  \begin{gathered}
  \exists j:\ c\in h[j].\ws \;\wedge\; h[j].\wt > h[i].\wt\\
  \qquad\wedge\; h[j].\wv[c]\neq v\\
  \wedge\ \forall k:\ (c\in h[k].\ws \wedge h[k].\wt > h[i].\wt)\\
  \qquad\qquad\Rightarrow h[k].\wt \le h[j].\wt.
  \end{gathered}
\]
\end{definition}

\A1 and \Rp{} partition ``an effect is out on a basis the state no longer
holds'' by whether the superseding write committed inside the operation's
window or after its commit; a value overwritten and then restored fires
neither, and \Rp{} carries no agent conjunct because the obligation is owed
whoever supersedes. On the live data the partition is exact
(\cref{tab:live}): $A_1 + R = 100$ in every unguarded cell, and under $L_1$
the \A1 column is empty and the residue is entirely \Rp{}. The two sides of
that identity are computed independently---the outcome column compares the
booking with the final store state and never reads a basis; \Rp{} reads the
trace's write order and never the final state---so the identity is a check
on the extractor, not a tautology of it; on this workload the extractor
recovers the basis of \HypBasisRecovered/\HypBookings{} bookings because the
booking tool takes the date as a structured argument, and its blind spot is
stated in \cref{sec:limitations}. TLC's witness has five states and is not
an \A1 witness: an operation reads $c_1=\mathrm{NULL}$ and commits an effect
with basis $\{(c_1,\mathrm{NULL})\}$, released at commit; a later operation
commits $c_1=v_1$.

\begin{proposition}[What remains after release]
\label{prop:owed}
Let $D$ be any discipline under which an effect $e$ with basis $B$ is
released at a finite time $T$, and let the store accept, at any time after
$T$, a write to a cell of $B$ by an operation that does not depend on $e$
(the open-world hypothesis). (i)~There is an execution of $D$ in which a
write committed after $T$ supersedes $B$ while $e$ is out. (ii)~If
obligations are never cleared, $\neg R$ holds in every reachable state of
$D$ if and only if, in every execution, $e$'s obligation is set in the same
step as, or before, the step that completes the other conjuncts of
\cref{def:residue}---the superseding commit for a released effect, the
release for an effect superseded while held.
\end{proposition}
Both halves are immediate---(i) because the write is admitted after a
release that cannot be recalled, (ii) because \cref{def:residue} is
``superseded and not owed'' and a monotone flag set by that step stays
set---and we record them as a proposition, not a theorem, because their
content is to fix what a discipline can promise about \Rp{} under the
open-world hypothesis: not a current basis, but an obligation registered
atomically with the step that completes the witness, which the ledger of
\cref{sec:ledger} does at both steps. Dropping the hypothesis is possible
and is not free: freezing the cells of $B$ until the effect is acknowledged
is a lock held past commit, with the pessimistic column of \cref{tab:cost}
as its price floor; output commit (\cref{sec:a3}) holds effects only until
their \emph{causal} basis is externalized, a finite condition. What a
registered obligation buys in the world---a rebooking, a cancellation, a
human notification---is a property of the compensator and is measured, not
proved (\cref{sec:live-a1}).

\section{The lattice and one chain through it}
\label{sec:lattice}

A consistency model is a predicate over histories, characterized by the set of
anomalies it excludes~\cite{Adya1999}. With
$\mathcal{A}=\{A_1,A_2,A_3,A_6\}$ the design space is the Boolean lattice
$\langle 2^{\mathcal{A}},\subseteq\rangle$: sixteen points, bottom
$\emptyset$ admitting everything, top $\mathcal{A}$ admitting nothing
(\cref{fig:lattice}). We name five points on one maximal chain,
\[
  L_0\equiv\emptyset,\;
  L_1\equiv\{A_1\},\;
  L_2\equiv\{A_1,A_3\},\;
  L_3\equiv\{A_1,A_3,A_6\},\;
  L_4\equiv\mathcal{A},
\]
chosen on operational grounds (\A1 first because it is the basic
read--generate--commit failure; \A2 last because registry stability across
the operation lifetime is the costliest single requirement); the other
twenty-three linearizations are equally valid and the lattice, not the chain,
is primary. The residue predicate \Rp{} is not a generator: it is not an
anomaly, and its answer, the basis ledger $\ell$ of \cref{sec:ledger},
composes with any point.

\subsection{What is and is not a result}
The chain's inclusions are definitional and its strictness needs one witness
per step; neither is a result. What requires work is \emph{realizability}: a
runtime configuration that excludes exactly each point's anomalies, and for
the five named points a verified realizing runtime---established here by
bounded model checking, and claimed at the bounds stated.

\subsection{All sixteen points, mechanically; the ledger composes}
\label{sec:lattice16}
\texttt{Guarded.tla} extends the runtime with four commit-time disciplines,
each enabled by a Boolean constant and each acting on its own carrier: $G_1$,
read-set validation (an operation whose read set was overwritten after its
read may not commit); $G_2$, registry validation (a planned tool no longer in
the registry may not be dispatched); $G_3$, causal tracking with output commit
(an operation records the closure of committed writers whose values it read,
may not commit while any is aborted, externalizes only once its closure has
externalized, and once externalized is irrevocable; a retraction cascades to
every unexternalized dependent); $G_6$, commit-order sequencing (effects
externalize in issuance order). The carriers are disjoint---read set and write
times; registry and planned tool; closure and abort flags; issuance and commit
orders---so enabling one guard neither creates nor removes another anomaly,
and the matrix confirms it on every combination.

For each of the sixteen guard sets $S$ and each anomaly $a$, TLC checks
$R(S)\models\neg a$: for $a\in S$ the invariant must hold exhaustively, for
$a\notin S$ it must be violated. All \LatticeRuns{} runs match
(\texttt{lattice16.sh}; $|A|{=}2$, $|\fld{Cells}|{=}2$, $|\fld{Values}|{=}1$,
$\fld{MaxOps}{=}2$, symmetry on agents and cells; the $32$ safety runs are
exhaustive over \LatticeSafeStatesMin{}--\LatticeSafeStatesMax{} distinct
states). Because \textsc{CausalCascade} ranges over flags and predecessor
sets and never over cells, the sixteen \textsc{NoA3} points are rechecked at
$\fld{MaxOps}{=}3$ with one cell, where a transitive cascade first becomes
reachable: \LatticeMaxOpsThreeRuns{}/\LatticeMaxOpsThreeRuns{} match. A
single-value domain makes \cref{def:a1}'s value conjunct vacuous on non-null
reads; the safety half re-run at $|\fld{Values}|{=}2$ stays safe in all
$32$ cells, and a targeted check shows the non-null-read witness class needs
both a second value \emph{and} a third operation (supplement \S\,L); the
two-value safety half is re-run on the current model (\LatticeVtwoOutcome).
Every point is thus inhabited by a runtime that prevents exactly its
avoidance set, at these bounds; the Verus product of the $L_2$, $L_3$ and
$L_4$ models (\cref{sec:composition}) mechanizes the top point without a
bound.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{The ledger composes.} \texttt{Guarded5.tla} adds a
fifth constant $G_7$, the basis ledger of \cref{sec:ledger}, which refuses
nothing and writes only the basis and obligation fields.
\texttt{lattice32.sh} runs all \LatticeThirtyTwoRuns{} combinations of the
five constants against the four anomalies and \Rp{} at the same bounds. The
$64$ anomaly cells with $G_7$ on return the verdicts of \texttt{lattice16.sh}
one for one, so the ledger disturbs no point of the lattice; \Rp{} is
reached without $G_7$ in a five-state trace at every point and excluded
with $G_7$ at every point (exhaustive; \NoAsevenStates{} distinct states at
the bottom point, \NoAsevenStatesGthree{} with output commit, where a held
effect can be superseded before its release). \LatticeThirtyTwoOutcome{}

\subsection{Verified detectors, and one transcription gap}
\label{sec:verus-detectors}
The four detectors are verified sound \emph{and} complete against Verus
\texttt{spec} restatements of the \TLA{} predicates (\VerusDetector{}
obligations). \texttt{detect\_a1} is a two-sided equivalence whose
completeness half strengthens each of three nested loop invariants to carry
``no witness in the prefix searched.'' \texttt{detect\_a2} and
\texttt{detect\_a6} are single-record equivalences; \texttt{detect\_a3} is
exact for the flat-trace residue of \cref{sec:a3}.

The restatement is hand-written and checked by inspection, and inspection
found a gap we state rather than hide. The Verus \texttt{OpRecord} carries no
agent field, so its \A1 predicate omits the different-agent conjunct of
\cref{def:a1}; and it decides ``no witness'' when a read value is absent,
where the \TLA{} compares against $\mathrm{NULL}$. Neither matters on the
histories the model admits, and we say why.

\begin{lemma}[Same-agent exclusion]
\label{lem:same-agent}
Under per-agent non-overlap and read-before-commit, if
$c\in h[i].\rs\cap h[k].\ws$ and $h[i].\rt<h[k].\wt<h[i].\wt$, then
$h[i].\fld{agent}\neq h[k].\fld{agent}$.
\end{lemma}
The lemma is step $\langle2\rangle2$ of the TLAPS proof of \cref{thm:corc}
and the content of \texttt{A1LowerBound.tla}. It makes the Verus predicate
and \cref{def:a1} coincide on every history satisfying the model's invariants;
on a history that violates non-overlap the Verus predicate is strictly
stronger---it over-reports, the safe direction for a detector. The
instrumentation records a value for every read cell, so the absent-value case
does not arise: across all \HypRecords{} committed operation records there
is no read cell without a value and \HypSameAgentOverlaps{} same-agent
overlapping windows (\texttt{hypothesis\_gates.py --check}). The published tables were
scored by \texttt{rust-analyser}, a hand-written port of the verified
detector; a gate (\texttt{predicate\_matrix\_a1.py}) scores every committed
cell under the \TLA{}, the Verus and the port's transcription and fails on
any divergence, and all three agree on every cell. Wiring the verified
\texttt{detect\_a1} into the scorer is a build step the artifact's delegation
shim prepares and this version has not taken.

\subsection{$L_1$: two concurrent stores whose lock is verified, not assumed}
\label{sec:l1}
Three abstract state machines---pessimistic locking, serializable snapshot
isolation (SSI), and default snapshot isolation with its no-write bypass---are
proved \A1-free under sequential interleaving ($40$ obligations; default-SI
conditionally, on workloads with no read-only commit, which is the residual
\cref{sec:pilot} measures). The deployed stores do not rest on these: each is
an exec-mode store whose only access path is \texttt{vstd::rwlock::RwLock},
the reader--writer lock implemented and verified inside the Verus standard
library, carrying the store's invariant as the lock predicate
(\texttt{lib\_si\_concurrent.rs}, $18$; \texttt{lib\_pess\_concurrent.rs},
$31$; no axiom, no \texttt{external\_body}). The invariant entails the absence
of any stale-generation window in the store's own record stream, under any
interleaving of threads. Theorem L1j (\texttt{lib\_si\_projection.rs}, $3$)
transports that guarantee to the detector's history for \emph{faithful}
emitters---one \texttt{OpRecord} per store record, cells a subset of the
record's. The default-SI \texttt{tick} path is the standing counterexample to
faithfulness and is exactly the $\PilotSiEditResidual\%$/$\PilotSiTriageResidual\%$
residual of \cref{tab:pilot}. Four earlier refinements ($86$ obligations,
two axioms) relate the abstract machines to the representations the runtime
shipped \emph{before} the verified-lock rebuild and bear no deployed claim;
refining the two concurrent stores to the abstract machines is the open
proof step (a bounded simulation over $506{,}880$ transitions finds no
counterexample; supplement \S\,H).

\subsection{$L_2$: output commit}
\label{sec:l2}
The $L_2$ model (\texttt{lib\_l2\_safety.rs}, $33$) carries per transaction a
predecessor set recorded as a causal closure at read time. A commit is valid
iff the read set is fresh against the committed store and every predecessor
is committed and unaborted; an abort cascades to every transaction whose
closure holds the aborter; effects leave by a separate \texttt{externalize}
step enabled only once the closure has externalized; an externalized
transaction cannot abort. Theorem $L_2$h: no state satisfying the inductive
invariant $\fld{inv\_output\_commit}$ contains a witness of \cref{def:a3}
(\texttt{lemma\_l2\_reachable\_no\_a3}), under arbitrary interleaving of the
six steps; the closure invariant makes the one-level cascade provably
sufficient for the transitive case. Theorem $L_2$i: every committed read has
a surviving producer of exactly its value, and (temporal strengthening) that
producer committed no later than the read. Theorem $L_2$j
(\texttt{lib\_l2\_projection.rs}, $3$): a faithful emission of an
$L_2$-supported state contains no residue witness, by a bridge that never
refers to a read's producer and so cannot assume what it proves. The exec
runtime (\texttt{lib\_l2\_exec.rs}, $73$ end to end, $40$ net of the
re-included model) refines the model per transition with zero added axioms
(its \texttt{u64} interning is natively injective); every mutating entry
point checks its own guard and reports whether it acted, so the guarantee
survives proof erasure under plain \texttt{cargo}, and the file compiled by
the reference runtime is byte-identical.

\subsection{$L_3$: sequencing, and $L_4$: validation at dispatch}
\label{sec:l3}\label{sec:l4}
Theorem $L_3$g (\texttt{lib\_l3\_sequencer.rs}, $5$): a commit-order
sequencer that externalizes effect $k$ once effects $0..k$ have completed
yields an externalization time monotone in issuance index for \emph{every}
completion schedule, hence no \A6, without forbidding concurrency; a
reversed schedule exhibits \A6 unsequenced and none sequenced. The saga
model (\texttt{lib\_l3\_safety.rs}, $6$) is the strict-serialization special
case and composes with $L_2$ on disjoint carriers. The exec sequencer
(\texttt{lib\_l3\_exec.rs}, $7$) is verified; its measurement
(\cref{sec:a6-eval}) runs a dependency-free twin, a gap the trust base
records.

Resolving a planned tool against a registry snapshot does not prevent \A2: a
snapshot changes what an operation resolves, not the tool its call reaches
(Theorem $L_4$d, a concrete execution). The $L_4$ model
(\texttt{lib\_l4\_safety.rs}, $9$) reads \A2 from a \emph{dispatch record}
and validates at dispatch---place the call only if the planned tool is still
live with the pinned signature; registry churn interleaves arbitrarily and
cannot create \A2 because \A2 is a property of the dispatch. The exec
dispatcher (\texttt{lib\_l4\_exec.rs}, $11$) refuses a revoked or re-signed
tool and dispatches an untouched one, and \cref{sec:l4-eval} measures those
bytes. Validation and dispatch are one atomic step of a registry the runtime
owns; a remote tool server (MCP~\cite{AnthropicMCP2024}) offers no such
atomicity and the check-then-call gap there is narrowed, not closed.


\subsection{The basis ledger}
\label{sec:ledger}
The ledger $\ell$ answers \Rp{} and composes with any discipline; the live
study runs it over $L_1$ (\cref{tab:live}). Its model
(\texttt{lib\_l5\_ledger.rs}, \LfiveVerus) carries the
store's committed values and, per effect, its basis map, an externalized
flag and an obligation flag. Three steps: \emph{register} commits an effect
with its basis (released at once, or held under output commit; if released
at once on a basis the store already disagrees with, the obligation is set
at commit); \emph{write} installs a value and marks, in the same step, every
externalized effect whose basis holds that cell with a different value;
\emph{externalize} releases a held effect and marks it if its basis is stale
by then. The invariant $\mathit{inv\_l5}$---every externalized effect with a
stale basis carries an obligation---is preserved by each step (the write
step is the load-bearing case: the cell that became stale is the cell the
step marked, and any other stale cell was stale before), and the ledger theorem:
no state satisfying $\mathit{inv\_l5}$ contains a witness of \cref{def:residue}.
The model's witness is the state form---an externalized, unowed effect whose
basis the store now disagrees with---which contains \cref{def:residue}'s (a
latest post-commit writer that differs implies a current value that
differs), so excluding it excludes the definition's witness and, in
addition, an effect released on a basis already stale at commit.
Non-vacuity in both directions: the unguarded write reaches \Rp{} in two
steps from an effect derived from an unwritten cell, and under the ledger the
same write registers the obligation while a write that restores the value
leaves no witness. The ledger refuses nothing; refusing to release a
stale-basis effect, or holding it for a deadline, is a policy the executable
runtime layers on top, and what a registered obligation achieves in the
world is measured in \cref{sec:live-a1}. The model is \LfiveVerusIntro{}
under the pinned build; its obligations are counted as scaffolding
(\VerusScaffold{} with it) because the executable ledger of the live study is
harness code, not these bytes. An exec ledger on the $L_2$/$L_4$ pattern is
not in this version.

\subsection{Composition}
\label{sec:composition}
\texttt{lib\_consistency\_lattice.rs} takes the product of the $L_2$, $L_3$
and $L_4$ states over their disjoint carriers and proves that the conjunction
of the three invariants excludes \A1 (image), \A3, \A6 and \A2 jointly, with
three non-interference lemmas; $53$ obligations, of which $48$ are the
re-verified component modules. The ledger's carrier (basis, obligation) is
disjoint from all three, and adding it to the product is the same
construction; it is not in this version.

\subsection{Trust base}
\label{sec:trust}
``Zero \texttt{assume}, zero \texttt{admit}'' is a statement about proof
bodies. What is trusted, in full: (i) Verus, its SMT backend and
\texttt{vstd}, including \texttt{group\_hash\_axioms} for the
\texttt{HashMap}-backed exec states; (ii) two axioms in the four superseded
refinements (string injectivity, null sentinel) and (iii) three
\texttt{external\_body} stubs for a \texttt{parking\_lot::Mutex}, neither
bearing a deployed claim since the verified-lock rebuild; (iv) the hand
restatement of the \TLA{} predicates in Verus, with the gap of
\cref{sec:verus-detectors} stated and gated; (v) the hand-written scorer that
produced the tables, gated against the verified predicate on every committed
cell; (vi) for $L_3$, the inspection-level correspondence between the exec
artifact and the measured twin; (vii) the \texttt{pub} fields of the exec
runtimes, which a caller can write around the API; (viii) the marshalling
that flattens the version map into the verified validation gate, and the
interning bijection and abort counter outside the verified store; (ix) the
Python and Rust instrumentation that produces every \texttt{OpRecord},
including the read sets it declares---a detector is as good as the read set
it is handed, and recovering implicit read sets is a separate
problem~\cite{Khan2026SBus}; (x) the provenance correspondence between the
two formalizations of \A3, which agree only under value-unique writes
(checked on every scored trace); (xi) in the live study's verified arm, the
$100$-line JSON bridge and the harness's interning of cells and values. No
safety-bearing proof invokes a writes-as-function-of-reads assumption, and
the development carries no probabilistic obligation.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

Three questions. \textbf{RQ1} For each discipline, what does it prevent, and
what harm remains once it has? \textbf{RQ2} What does prevention cost, per
unit of work the discipline actually delivered? \textbf{RQ3} Where do the
structural preconditions of the anomalies occur in deployed code? A verified
arm's zero is a theorem, so every zero below is reported beside a count that
reads no runtime flag.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Provenance.} Every table cell maps to a committed
trace directory and a scorer in the artifact; \texttt{reproduce.sh} rescores
all of them offline under an implementation of \cref{def:a1} independent of
the verified detector and the Rust port, and fails on any disagreement. The
numbers in this section were re-derived that way for this version.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{What $L_1$ removes, and what it does not.}
Read-set validation removed every firing ($0/300$) at one discarded
generation per realized conflict (\LiveGptRegen{} and \LiveHaikuRegen{} per
$100$ sessions, never two in one session, none where the race never
realized). It did not make the booking agree with the final trip date: in
$\LiveGptValidatedWrong$ and $\LiveHaikuValidatedWrong$ of $100$ sessions the
revision landed after the commit: the committed record is current at its
commit point exactly as \cref{thm:corc} says, and the booking is stale with
respect to a state that did not yet exist. That residue is a post-commit
supersession---the blind overwrite of \cref{sec:a1}, serializable in commit
order, outside every isolation discipline---and only compensation, or
holding the effect until its basis is final, reaches it. The
\emph{sequential} control, clean under every commit-time predicate, delivers
the wrong booking in $100/100$ sessions for the same reason; reporting the
predicate alone would have credited $L_1$ with a correctness it does not
provide.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{\Rp{} decides the residue on the records.}
\Cref{def:residue} names what is left, and the \Rp{} column of
\cref{tab:live} is decided on the emitted records alone: the basis of each
booking is the date value in its arguments, and \Rp{} fires in exactly the
sessions whose booking disagrees with the final date and \A1 does not
($A_1+R=100$ in every unguarded cell; under $L_1$ the \Rp{} column equals
the residue; \texttt{a7\_rescore.py -{}-check}). The identity is between two
independent computations (\cref{sec:residue}), and it holds here because
the booking tool takes the date as a structured argument; a workload whose
effects paraphrase their inputs would show a recall below
\HypBasisRecovered/\HypBookings{} and a smaller \Rp{} column, never a larger
one. Two further arms run the same harness on three API families (haiku,
gpt-4o-mini and deepseek-chat, the last through its OpenAI-compatible
endpoint). In the \emph{verified} arm the $L_1$ decision is taken by the
verified store through the bridge of \cref{sec:trust}: \A1 fires in $0/300$
sessions, and the residue is $2$, $51$ and $52$ of $100$---the same kind of
number the application-layer arm leaves in the same run ($12$, $51$, $63$),
the haiku pair differing by run-to-run sampling of which revisions land
after the commit, and the two runs of the validated arm on gpt-4o-mini ($34$
in September, $51$ in October) showing the same spread. In the \emph{ledger}
arm a superseding put registers the obligation and a compensator issues a
replacement booking: the outcome column is $0/100$ on all three families
($5$, $45$ and $53$ obligations registered, each discharged by one
compensation generation), at the price of the discarded generations of $L_1$
plus one generation per obligation.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{The offset sweep.} \Cref{fig:offset} varies the
race instead of fixing it: the date agent starts $0$, $150$, $500$ or
$1{,}500$\,ms after the flight agent's read and revises in about half the
sessions ($p=0.5$), $n=100$ per point on the three API families. Without a
discipline \A1 fires in $39$--$46$ of $100$ sessions at $0$\,ms and in $0$ at
$1{,}500$\,ms, where every revision lands after the commit, while the
booking is wrong in every revised session at every offset ($44$--$51$).
Under $L_1$ the curve inverts: the residue is $2$--$17$ at $0$\,ms and
$42$--$52$ at $500$--$1{,}500$\,ms, the discarded generations falling from
$34$--$49$ to $0$. Predicate and residue are the two halves of one quantity,
the revision rate, split by where the revision lands relative to the commit;
which half a deployment sees is a property of its latencies, and the ledger
is the only arm whose outcome does not depend on them. What
\cref{prop:owed} says must remain after release does remain under every
commit-time discipline, and the ledger is the one arm that answers it.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Superstep.} A second graph places a writer and a
summarizer of one reducer-declared channel in one Pregel
superstep~\cite{MalewiczEtAl2010Pregel}; the barrier hands both the snapshot
at superstep entry, so in $300/300$ sessions the summary names the
destination the writer had changed. This is the snapshot-insufficiency shape
of \cref{sec:snapshot} observed live (a reducer buys lost-update freedom,
not $L_1$), reported as documented BSP semantics, not a rate: the census
finds it in no public graph, and under a completion clock \cref{def:a1}
fires exactly when the writer's body returned first (\SuperstepGpt,
\SuperstepHaiku, \SuperstepLlama{} of $100$; \texttt{check\_clocks.sh}).

\subsection{RQ1 for $L_2$: output commit on synthetic cascades and live verdicts}
\label{sec:l2-eval}
Three arms run the same $1{,}000$ seeded schedules at nominal chain depths
$2$, $3$ and $5$: a root commits, a chain of dependents reads the previous
cell and commits, one committed transaction is put under review and retracted
or approved, and in a quarter of scenarios the review arrives after that
transaction has released. The \emph{verified} arm is \texttt{l2\_exec.rs};
the \emph{unguarded} arm validates reads, releases at commit and flags only
the aborted transaction; the \emph{cascade} arm releases at commit and
cascades the abort. Each trace is scored under \cref{def:a3}, under the
surviving-dependent predicate, and by \emph{exposed effects}---effects out at
the end although the review asked to withdraw their basis---which reads no
flag (full table: supplement \S\,P). The result is one structural fact.
\Cref{def:a3} fires in \LtwoDefThreeBaseline{} of $1{,}000$ schedules per
depth under both baselines and in none under the verified arm; the cascade
arm satisfies its own surviving-dependent predicate in every scenario while
exposing exactly as many effects as no discipline at all
(\LtwoExposedBaseline{}), because it flagged \LtwoRelabeled{} dependents
\emph{after} their effects were out---a cascade without output commit moves
flags, not effects. The verified arm is not zero on exposed effects:
\LtwoExposedVerified{} effects in $199$ scenarios per depth, exactly the
scenarios in which the review arrived after the release and the retraction
was refused. The figures decompose by arithmetic ($1{,}538 = 402 + 572 +
564$ at depth 2): the first term no runtime can remove; the second, one
reviewed operation per early review, any runtime that declines to release an
operation under review avoids; the third ($37$--$46\%$ of the unguarded
exposure) is output commit's own contribution, dependents whose release it
refused and the cascade then cancelled. Its cost is delay, not liveness:
$748$, $1{,}255$ and $1{,}272$ operations had a release refused at least
once, and every committed-and-unaborted operation was eventually released.
The quarter of late reviews is a harness parameter, so the overall exposure
reduction ($73$--$74\%$) is that parameter seen from the other side and is
not a finding.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Live verdicts.} A plan--execute--revise workload
driven by real models (a planner commits a plan; an executor reads it,
acquiring the planner as predecessor, and commits; a supervisor decides from
the ticket and plan whether to retract) was recorded for $200$ sessions on
each of three families and replayed through the three arms. Retraction rates
varied sharply---$1/200$ (claude-haiku-4-5), $3/200$ (gpt-4o-mini),
$62/200$ (Llama-3.2)---so we make the pooled statement only: in every one of
the \LiveLtwoRetractions{} retracted sessions the unguarded baseline and the
cascade design left a \cref{def:a3} witness (the latter while satisfying the
surviving-dependent predicate), and the verified runtime released no executor
effect on a retracted plan, at a pooled release rate of $89.0\%$. The zero is
structural; what the replay adds is that the discipline executes under
divergent live supervisor behavior at a stated cost.

\subsection{RQ1 for $L_4$: validation at dispatch}

with contention (supplement \S\,M).

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Wall-clock, with its confound.} The nine cells of
a run were executed one after another, so a difference between cells carries
whatever the provider's latency did in between. Plan-execute, on which no
runtime aborts, is the control, and its ``overheads'' are $-4.5\%$ and
$-4.9\%$ on gpt-4o (Holm-corrected permutation $p=0.014$, $0.002$) and
$+13.5\%$ for SI on Claude ($p=0.001$): that is drift, and we report its
largest magnitude as the run's null band. Outside it, on mean and median
alike: pessimistic locking at $+16.7\%$ (edit-review) and $+34.9\%$ (triage)
on gpt-4o and $+109.5\%$ (triage) on Claude, where it makes $7.75$ model
calls per session against $4.00$. Snapshot isolation is inside the band in
every cell on both families, which is not the same as zero: the design cannot
resolve an overhead below about $5\%$ on gpt-4o or $14\%$ on Claude, and the
per-sound-commit wall-clock overheads on gpt-4o ($+4.9\%$ and $+11.0\%$) sit
exactly there. Only a rerun with the three runtimes interleaved per session
removes the confound; probe~302 is that design, and its paired per-session
overheads (mean, bootstrap 95\% CI; ``--'' until run) are, on gpt-4o,
triage pessimistic \WallclockPairedGptTriagePess{} and SI
\WallclockPairedGptTriageSI, with the plan-execute control at
\WallclockPairedGptPlanExecutePess{} and \WallclockPairedGptPlanExecuteSI{}
(supplement \S\,O for every cell).

\subsection{Operational materiality of a firing}
\label{sec:materiality}
A value mismatch may be lexical. Holding an agent's context fixed and varying
only the shared value it read, the decision changed in $150/200$ flagged
mismatches of a controlled triage task ($75.0\%$ $[68.4, 80.8]$); divergence
tracks whether the value pair straddles a decision boundary, so the rate is
a property of the pairs sampled as much as of the agents, offered as the
paper's only value-staleness estimate, not a constant. On the pilot's
\cref{def:a1} firings (all cold-start reads by the reviewer) materiality is
$43/43$ under an LLM judge, blind-coded by hand on $15$; a human-judge
agreement of $59/60$ on a broader sample says the judge reads decision-level
divergence, not that those $43$ are validated.

\subsection{RQ3: where the preconditions occur}
\label{sec:prevalence}
Every occurrence figure above is a race we opened or a rate on our own
workloads. To put a denominator under the structural precondition of \A1 in
the framework we studied---a reader and a writer of one last-value channel
released into the same superstep---we analyzed public code. \emph{Frame}:
GitHub repositories matching \texttt{langgraph} in name, description or
readme (Python; forks and the framework's own organization excluded), in
three star bands; $60$ drawn per band under a fixed seed from the API's $500$
most recently updated. \emph{Instrument}: a static front end that recovers,
from the syntax tree alone, each \texttt{StateGraph} site, the node pairs one
fork releases into a superstep, and each node's read and written keys;
whatever it cannot resolve is \emph{unknown}, never guessed; eleven fixtures
pin its rules and a differential oracle finds no disagreement between its
pairs and the executed ones on the eight runnable fixtures.

probed, four close the window and one leaves it open: Letta re-renders the

of a concurrently produced value at construction; AutoGen's actors own their
state. LangGraph's superstep channels and cross-thread store pin a read across
a node body that may contain a model call, which is where \cref{sec:live-a1}
measures it. Five systems is not a sample; the value of the list is that each
entry names a mechanism a designer can choose. Two framework defects outside
the catalog were found on the way: the widely cited deer-flow bug (\#3123) is
a sequential blind overwrite under a last-value channel, not a concurrency
anomaly, and \texttt{interrupt}'s re-execution emits an effect issued before
the gate twice, with different arguments when the shared value was revised
during the gate (supplement \S\,Q).

\subsection{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}
\emph{Construct.} \A1 tracks the window, not the harm; \cref{tab:live}
reports both, the gap is the paper's own finding, and \Rp{} is its name. The
basis is argument provenance, verbatim and not semantic. Whether a booking
that disagrees with a later revision is harm is the application's call; we
count it because the user asked for one trip. \A6 fires on commuting
effects and is silent on dropped ones. \emph{Internal.} The live rates are
latency races, $n=100$ single runs per cell, at one offset in \cref{tab:live}
and four in \cref{fig:offset}; the wall-clock cells carry a run-order
confound we bound rather than remove (probe~302 removes it; not run); the L2
live replay rests on $66$ retractions, $62$ from one family. \emph{External.} Three API families and one local; one
framework driven live; toy workloads of two to three agents. \emph{Reproducibility.} Every cell is
committed with its traces and rescored by an independent implementation of
\cref{def:a1}; run \texttt{20260913T0424Z} carries a manifest captured at run
time, the two earlier runs carry reconstructed manifests that say so.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Classical consistency and isolation}
The hardware tradition~\cite{Lamport1979SC,GharachorlooEtAl1990,PulteEtAl2018ARMv8,HerlihyWing1990}
characterizes visibility under bounded latency; ours is unbounded. The
database isolation
hierarchy~\cite{BerensonEtAl1995,Adya1999,AdyaLiskovOneil2000} is the direct
ancestor: we adopt its anomaly-based method over a different operational
model. The line through Fekete et al.~\cite{FeketeEtAl2005MakingSI} and
Cahill et al.~\cite{CahillRohmFekete2008SSI}, productized in
PostgreSQL~\cite{PortsGrittner2012}, is the reference for our SSI runtime; the
difference is that the work discarded on abort is an inference. Weikum and
Vossen's commit-order-preserving serializability~\cite{WeikumVossen2001} and
Kung and Robinson's backward validation~\cite{KungRobinson1981OCC} are what
\cref{thm:corc} mechanizes at the level of values read; Papadimitriou's view
equivalence~\cite{Papadimitriou1979} is where the value-level and
conflict-level notions meet. Bailis et al.'s highly available
transactions~\cite{BailisEtAl2014HAT} classify isolation levels by the
anomalies they admit under availability constraints, the taxonomy our lattice
borrows its method from; Cerone, Bernardi and
Gotsman~\cite{CeroneEtAl2015Framework} give the axiomatic framework our
history predicates specialize; \emph{robustness}---whether every execution of
an application under a weak model is
serializable~\cite{BernardiGotsman2016Robustness,BeillahiEtAl2019RobustnessSI}---is
what the census of \cref{sec:prevalence} asks statically; our detectors
are polynomial~\cite{BiswasEnea2019Complexity} only because each predicate is
existential over a constant number of records. Crooks et
al.~\cite{CrooksEtAl2017Seeing} give the client-centric formulation we do
not adopt. Compensation descends from
sagas~\cite{GarciaMolinaSalem1987Sagas}, ConTracts~\cite{ReuterWachter1991ConTract}
and Gray and Reuter~\cite{GrayReuter1993}; recoverability and strictness
are Hadzilacos~\cite{Hadzilacos1988Reliability} and Bernstein et
al.~\cite{BernsteinHadzilacosGoodman1987}. Irrevocable transactions~\cite{WelcEtAl2008Irrevocable} and transactional
boosting~\cite{HerlihyKoskinen2008Boosting} met the same irreversibility in
transactional memory; output commit is the dual choice, delay the effect and
keep the operation abortable. Commutativity-based concurrency
control~\cite{Weihl1988Commutativity} orders only what does not commute, and
an \A6 discipline over commutativity classes rather than issuance order
would pay the sequencing cost only where it buys something; transaction
chopping~\cite{ShashaEtAl1995Chopping} says when a long operation may be
split, the question an agent's multi-step plan raises. The basis of \cref{def:residue} is
why-provenance~\cite{BunemanEtAl2001Why,GreenEtAl2007Semirings} restricted
to copying; that literature's treatment of derivation through computation
is what a semantic basis would need. Exactly-once stream
processing~\cite{AkidauEtAl2013MillWheel,CarboneEtAl2017Flink} holds
side effects at a transactional sink until the input is durable, the
industrial precedent for output commit. The pessimistic/optimistic choice our cost
analysis turns on is the contention-management problem of
STM~\cite{GuerraouiHerlihyPochon2005,SchererScott2005}, and the cost-aware
selection of a level is consistency
rationing~\cite{KraskaEtAl2009Rationing} and, in databases today,
TxnSails~\cite{ZhouEtAl2025TxnSails}.

\subsection{Concurrency control and shared memory for LLM agents}
A 2025--2026 line builds mechanisms for this regime, mostly without verifying
them, and the present paper is one point in it rather than its origin.
Agentic transactions~\cite{SunEtAl2026AgenticTx} recast ACID as four
semantic guarantees for a single long-horizon agent (explore--execute--validate
cycles, dependency-aware isolation) and measure task success, with no history
predicate, model or proof; our catalog is the anomaly-level account such
guarantees would be checked against, and supplement \S\,D places it on the
lattice with the systems below.
CoAgent~\cite{LyuEtAl2026CoAgent} states the regime in our three terms and
proposes an advisory protocol in which a notified agent repairs its own plan;
its correctness argument is conditional on a model judgment measured at a
$5\%$ failure rate, and its lock/OCC cost inversion independently reproduces
\cref{sec:cost}. STORM~\cite{LiuEtAl2026STORM} is read validation ($L_1$)
deployed per file; ATCC~\cite{ZhouEtAl2026ATCC}, OptiMA~\cite{Calikyilmaz2025OptiMA},
token coherence~\cite{Parakhin2026TokenCoherence} and GoEX~\cite{PatilEtAl2024GoEX}
impose a remedy from the runtime. MemTX~\cite{LiEtAl2026MemTX} is the closest
design to our $L_2$: staged writes under snapshot isolation, irreversible
tool calls gated on current belief and cascading repair on retraction, with
two invariants checked by bounded enumeration ($5.5$M states) and a
five-backbone evaluation; it does not state the predicates it excludes,
verify the runtime, or price prevention per unit of sound work. Yang et
al.~\cite{YangEtAl2026Position} argue that coordination failures attributed
to communication are concurrency failures; governed shared
memory~\cite{MargalitEtAl2026Governed} and transactive
memory~\cite{Transactive2026} address provenance, supersession and scoping
without treating the generation window as a transaction. SagaLLM~\cite{GengChang2025SagaLLM}, Atomix~\cite{MohammadiEtAl2026Atomix}
and CodeCRDT~\cite{Pugachev2025CodeCRDT} are placed on the lattice informally
in supplement \S\,D; none reaches $L_4$. S-Bus~\cite{Khan2026SBus} (the
author's prior work) recovers implicit read sets for one contract and is the
natural supplier of trust-base item (ix). Agent-memory systems~\cite{PackerEtAl2023MemGPT} and the
frameworks~\cite{LangGraph2026,WuEtAl2023AutoGen} supply substrate, not
contract.


\subsection{Commit-time guards, retraction, and what comes after commit}
\label{sec:related-residue}
The 2026 work on agent effects acts at the commit boundary:
CommitGuard~\cite{SantosGrueiro2026CommitGuard} licenses a durable effect
only by a witness still fresh at commit; Zheng et
al.~\cite{ZhengEtAl2026Stale} show that freshness guards of our $L_1$ kind
block $92$--$95\%$ of benign infrastructure races and argue for semantic
commit predicates; EffectMatch~\cite{ZhangEtAl2026EffectMatch} validates
persistent outcomes against what was approved; versioned
execution~\cite{ZhangEtAl2026Revisable} revokes an obsolete execution's
authority to publish. MemTX~\cite{LiEtAl2026MemTX} alone looks past
commit: on an explicit \emph{retraction} it walks a derivation DAG to
executed tool actions and records each as compensated or leaked. None treats the ordinary case \cref{tab:live}
measures---no retraction, no race, a legitimate later write---and none
states what guarantee is achievable there; \cref{prop:owed} says it is an
obligation under an open-world store, and the ledger registers it
atomically. Argument-level provenance is
used by PACT~\cite{FanEtAl2026PACT} to decide whether a tool argument's
origin is trusted; we use the same granularity for a different question,
which value an effect depends on, and the precision objection of Zheng et
al.\ is answered at the same place: a basis predicate ignores writes to
cells the effect never depended on.

\subsection{Asynchronous tool calling and effect order}
Parallel function calling is pursued deliberately:
LLMCompiler~\cite{KimEtAl2024LLMCompiler} plans a dependency graph and
dispatches independent calls concurrently, and asynchronous function
calling~\cite{GimEtAl2024AsyncLM,FutureAsync2026} lets a model issue calls
and continue generating while they complete, with ordering of effects left to
the runtime. \A6 is a wrong independence judgment seen at the effect level
(\cref{sec:a6-eval}: made by the backbone, unnoticed by the executor). The run-to-run spread the
evaluation reports is the documented nondeterminism of sampled
inference~\cite{AtilEtAl2024Stability}. Sandboxes and policies for tool
effects~\cite{RuanEtAl2024ToolEmu,DebenedettiEtAl2024AgentDojo,ShiEtAl2025Progent,WangEtAl2025AgentSpec,WuEtAl2025IsolateGPT}
gate \emph{what} an agent may do, orthogonally to the order and basis of
effects; $\tau$-bench~\cite{YaoEtAl2024TauBench} scores end state for a
single agent and isolates neither.

\subsection{Durable execution, exactly-once and repair}
Durable-execution engines~\cite{TemporalIO,BurckhardtEtAl2021Durable} and
exactly-once workflow
systems~\cite{SkiadopoulosEtAl2022DBOS,ZhangEtAl2020Beldi,JiaWitchel2021Boki,QiEtAl2023Halfmoon}
govern whether an effect is \emph{repeated} after a crash; output commit
governs whether it may \emph{leave} while its basis can be withdrawn, and the
two compose. Rollback
recovery~\cite{StromYemini1985Optimistic,ElnozahyEtAl2002Rollback}
discharges duplicates by identity because replay is deterministic; agent
runtimes break that premise~\cite{ZhengEtAl2026ACRFence}, and the
\texttt{interrupt} hazard of \cref{sec:prevalence} is an instance. Retro,
Warp and Aire~\cite{KimEtAl2010Retro,ChandraEtAl2011Warp,ChandraEtAl2013Aire}
repair effects that have already left by compensating requests; a refused
retraction in our $L_2$ is such an effect, and the recorded closure is the
dependency information they consume.

\subsection{Verified systems and black-box checkers}
IronFleet~\cite{HawblitzelEtAl2015IronFleet}, Verdi~\cite{WilcoxEtAl2015Verdi}
and Chapar~\cite{LesaniBellChlipala2016Chapar} verify running protocols and
stores; Perennial, GoJournal and
Argosy~\cite{ChajedEtAl2019Perennial,ChajedEtAl2021GoJournal,ChajedEtAl2019Argosy}
verify concurrent, crash-safe storage by layered refinement, the template
for the open step of \cref{sec:l1}. Ours are far smaller, and what they add is a verified detector and
verified bytes executed in a measurement.
Jepsen~\cite{Kingsbury2013Jepsen} and the black-box
checkers~\cite{TanEtAl2020Cobra,Aphyr2020Elle,HuangEtAl2023PolySI,ZhangEtAl2023Viper,LiuEtAl2024Plume,ClarkEtAl2024Emme}
decide serializability or snapshot isolation over database histories with
unverified analyzers under a uniqueness assumption we share (\cref{sec:a3});
ours decide a far simpler property and are verified against it, in the
tradition of runtime
verification~\cite{HavelundRosu2002,LeuckerSchallhart2009,BauerLeuckerSchallhart2011}.

\subsection{Agent failure taxonomies and verification}
MAST~\cite{CemriEtAl2025MAST} catalogs cognitive and coordination failures;
the concurrency class is absent because current benchmarks do not stress
shared state under contention, and its traces cannot show \A1 either way
(supplement \S\,C). Agentproof~\cite{XavierEtAl2026Agentproof} analyzes
agent graphs statically with witness generation; the census is that
programme specialized to one predicate (per-node write sets are not
statically derivable in LangGraph).
VeriMAP~\cite{Xu2025VeriMAP}, AgentVerify~\cite{AgentVerify2026} and
MCMAS~\cite{LomuscioQuRaimondi2017MCMAS} verify task-level and strategic
properties, a complementary layer.

\section{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\noindent\emph{The predicates are coarser than the phenomena.} \A1
over-reports in one shape (a value overwritten and restored inside the
window; \HypRestored{} of \HypAOneWitnesses{} witness pairs on the live
records) and, as \cref{tab:live} shows, under-reaches the harm by the
post-commit supersession that \Rp{} names. \A6 fires on commuting effects
and is blind to dropped and duplicated ones. The detectors are sound and
complete against their Verus specifications; \cref{tab:assurance} says for
each where the specification and the catalog definition coincide and under
what hypothesis.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{\Rp{} is not an anomaly, and its basis is
syntactic.} \Rp{} fires in $100/100$ sequential sessions because the
workload revises the date in every session; what \cref{tab:live}
establishes is that the harm an isolation discipline cannot reach is exactly
this predicate, not that concurrency causes it, which is why it stands
outside the catalog. Its basis is argument provenance: a value that appears
verbatim in an effect's arguments. A booking derived from the date by
computation (``the day after'') has an empty basis and is invisible, so the
extractor under-reaches and the \Rp{} column is a lower bound; on this
workload its recall is \HypBasisRecovered/\HypBookings{} because the booking
tool takes the date as a structured argument, and a workload with free-text
effects needs a declared or taint-tracked basis. Whether a registered
obligation is discharged, and at what cost, is a property of the
compensator; the ledger's guarantee ends at registration, and the ledger arm
of \cref{tab:live} measures one compensator on one workload.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{A refused retraction is not a prevented anomaly.}
Output commit makes \cref{def:a3} unreachable partly by holding effects and
partly by refusing to retract an operation whose effects are out; the second
leaves the world as it was and is counted (\cref{sec:l2-eval}). The
exposure--latency curve between the harness's one point and the live
driver's extreme is unmeasured.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Verification is relative to the trace, and the
triangle does not yet close on one system.} Trust-base item (ix) is the
largest: a stale read the instrumentation did not declare is invisible, and
recovering implicit read sets is a separate problem~\cite{Khan2026SBus}. The
scorer is a hand-written port gated against the verified predicate, not the
predicate itself; the refinement from the two concurrent stores to their
abstract machines is open; $L_3$ is measured on a twin; the $L_2$--$L_4$
exec artifacts are sequential objects a multi-threaded caller must
serialize. The verified arm puts verification and the flagship measurement
on one system for $L_1$ only; the ledger arm's executable is harness code.
Liveness is verified nowhere.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{At-most-once externalization is assumed, and a
deployed path breaks it.} LangGraph's \texttt{interrupt} resumes by
re-executing the node, which closes the stale-generation window and emits an
effect issued before the gate twice, with different arguments when the shared
value was revised during the gate; the catalog has no predicate for
duplication and we record it as a limitation of the model.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Prevalence is not established, and the census
points the other way.} The census bounds a structural precondition in public
code (none confirmed; at most about half counting every unresolved site)
and says nothing about private deployments, run frequency or the
cross-thread store variant; its reading is that public LangGraph code
rarely contends. Whether these anomalies occur in production at a rate
that justifies the costs of \cref{sec:cost} is open; the live rates are
latency races from single runs of $n=100$ sessions per point.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Not yet run}, each a step of the artifact's runner:
a multi-run repetition of the live cells; the interleaved cost rerun
(probe~302); the Claude pilot's guarded cells with operation records; and
wiring the verified detector into the scorer. Longer tasks, more agents and
an instrumented deployment remain the empirical follow-up; the store
refinements, the formal one.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Four classical consistency conditions port to the regime in which an agent's
step spans an inference call, and the development is mechanized at stated
assurance levels: \TLA{} predicates with TLC witnesses, a realizability
matrix over every point of their exclusion lattice, a TLAPS theorem placing
stale-generation freedom as a conditional commit-order read consistency,
Verus detectors sound and complete against their specifications with each
specification's relation to its definition named, and four disciplines
verified in Verus, two of them executed as the bytes that were verified.
What the four leave is not an anomaly: an effect out on a basis later
writes revise, which under an open-world store can only be owed, and for
which a verified basis ledger registers the obligation atomically at every
lattice point and, with a compensator, removes the residue on three API
families. The evaluation's contribution is not the verified arms' zeros, which are
theorems, but what sits beside them: the harm each discipline leaves (\Rp{}
in $34\%$ and $7\%$ of live sessions under $L_1$ at $150$\,ms and up to
$52\%$ as the race offset grows; $402$--$549$
exposed effects per $1{,}000$ cascades under $L_2$), its cost per unit of
sound work ($+0.4$--$11\%$ for snapshot isolation, up to $+97\%$ for
locking), and where the preconditions occur in deployed code (nowhere
confirmed in $407$ public LangGraph sites for \A1; $30/30$ turns of one
backbone's tool batching for \A6). A discipline should be judged by all three; the artifact re-derives each
from committed data.
